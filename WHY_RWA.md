If you've always felt that "investor" is a word for other people, that's not a personal failing; the entry barriers were real.

So the question isn't *whether* real assets are worth owning — you already suspect they are. It's **which pipe**: a bar in a safe, an ETF at the bank, an apartment with a mortgage — or a token. Here's the case for the token, in numbers and without stories.

## Gold: what you actually get through each pipe

Say you want to put $500 into gold.

- **A physical coin:** you'll pay a premium of several percent (up to double digits on small coins) over the market price, and you'll need a safe. Selling means going back to a dealer — with another spread.
- **An ETF:** you need a brokerage account; you're buying a *fund's promise*, not gold of your own; and you pay a management fee every single year — about 0.4%, which sounds small until you notice that over a 15-year horizon it quietly eats roughly 6% of your savings, regardless of how gold performs.
- **A gold-backed token (e.g., PAXG):** you buy exactly $500 — or $50 — in minutes. What you receive is a claim on allocated physical gold: a serial-numbered bar in an insured vault, from an issuer supervised by a US financial regulator that publishes periodic third-party attestations. The purchase fee is one-time (under 1% on the cheap route; more by credit card — and we show you exactly how much on every route). From then on: **zero management fees, forever.**

And three things only this pipe gives you:

1. **It's yours, with you.** Not an entry in an institution's ledger — an asset in a wallet you control. No broker who can freeze it, no trading hours.
2. **Liquid 24/7, globally.** Saturday night? You can sell. Moving countries? Your gold moves with you, nothing to re-open.
3. **Start genuinely small.** There's no "worthwhile minimum" — your first step can be $50, just to learn the process.

## Real estate: rental income without a mortgage and without tenants

The usual road to income real estate is a six-figure down payment, a mortgage, and tenants calling when the boiler dies. A REIT solves that — but you get an anonymous basket that trades like a stock and moves with the stock market.

Tokenized real estate opens a third road: **a specific house, on a specific street, that you picked** — for $50–$100 a token. Your share of the rent arrives on a regular schedule (on some platforms, daily), and operations sit with a local manager. This isn't "investing in the real-estate sector"; it's fractional ownership of one particular property with an address.

Honesty requires saying: this pipe is younger than the gold one. Ownership passes through a legal wrapper (an LLC), the secondary market is thin, and most platforms require signup and identity verification. That's exactly why our catalog marks the whole domain 🟡 and translates each property's legal wrapper into plain language — before you put in a cent.

## "But this is crypto, and I don't trust crypto"

Let's be precise, because this deserves a straight answer. **Yes — these assets live on the blockchain.** And that's not something to hide; it's exactly where the flexibility comes from. The blockchain is what makes it possible to buy a $50 fraction instead of a whole bar, to trade on a Saturday night, to hold the asset yourself instead of trusting a broker's ledger, and to move it anywhere in minutes. **Crypto's flexibility — with real-world representation:** behind each token stands something physical and verifiable — vaulted gold, a deeded house.

What you're *not* buying is speculation: no meme coins, nothing whose value derives from tweets. And the risks worth worrying about aren't "crypto" either — they're old, familiar questions: who is the issuer, who holds the asset, what are the redemption terms, who audits and when. Those are questions that can and must be answered from documents — and that's precisely what we do for you, token by token, with automatic red flags when something is off.

## When you should *not* do this

We only earn your trust if we say this part too:

- If you invest through a pension or other tax-advantaged account — the ETF's tax wrapper is probably worth more than the fee savings.
- If you need broad diversification in one click and zero involvement — a diversified fund beats a single asset.
- If this money is your safety cushion for the next six months — tokenized real estate, with its thin liquidity, is not the place.

This is not investment advice — we're not advisors and we don't recommend any asset. We're research: we put the facts in front of you, in your language, and you decide.
